# Epicast — High-Fidelity 2D Animation R&D (Druid Artwork V2)

## Objective

Develop an experimental, high-fidelity bitmap animation pipeline for the supplied Druid artwork, intended for the Epicast World of Warcraft: Forever addon.

Our visual inspiration is MapleStory's fluid, charming, hand-crafted 2D sprite animations, elevated with modern resolution, smooth movement and detailed painted textures.

We want the original illustration to come alive through subtle, organic motion.

**This is a standalone R&D experiment. Do not modify Epicast's production code, approved assets, existing animations or SavedVariables.**

## 1. Analyse the supplied artwork

The image features:

- Two large, symmetrical antler-like wooden structures at the upper left and right
- Twisting brown vines wrapping around the antlers and central stone
- Hanging moss and small foliage clusters
- A circular green tree symbol near the top centre
- A large green leaf emblem within a blue stone centrepiece
- Two side panels containing smaller leaf emblems
- Three bronze circular medallions along the lower portion
- A pink, star-shaped flower within the central medallion
- A hanging cluster of overlapping green leaves and curling vines at the bottom

The illustration has a transparent background and an intricate, painterly fantasy-game aesthetic.

The source image must remain the visual authority. Do not replace, simplify or reinterpret its details.

## 2. Investigate the animation technique

Determine the most effective way to produce high-quality, frame-based 2D bitmap animations from the original artwork.

Evaluate:

- Python/OpenCV mesh deformation
- Piecewise affine or thin-plate spline warping
- 2D skeletal animation
- AI-assisted segmentation
- Automated background reconstruction
- Deterministic keyframe interpolation

Blender may be considered for 2D compositing or rigging, but 3D modelling is not required.

The final output must consist of actual bitmap frames suitable for WoW sprite-sheet playback.

Avoid generative AI redraws of individual animation frames.

## 3. Experiment A — Hanging foliage animation

**Target:** The large hanging green leaf cluster beneath the three bronze medallions.

Create a gentle, organic swaying animation.

Desired behaviour:

1. Begin in the exact original resting pose.
2. Individual leaves gradually bend and sway.
3. Upper leaves move subtly while lower leaves move more noticeably.
4. Overlapping leaves move with slightly different timing.
5. The cluster settles naturally back into its original pose.

Duration: 1.6 seconds.

Target: 32 frames at 20 FPS.

Use deformation meshes rather than simple rigid rotation wherever practical.

The leaves should bend and flex while preserving their original painted textures and vein details.

Do not move the bronze medallions or surrounding wooden frame.

## 4. Experiment B — Hanging moss reaction

**Target:** The hanging moss strands around the upper-central stone and twisting vines.

Create a short, responsive flutter animation that could be triggered by a spell cast.

Desired behaviour:

1. Begin with the moss completely still.
2. A gentle wave travels through the hanging strands.
3. Individual strands bend and lag behind one another.
4. The movement briefly intensifies.
5. The strands gradually settle back into their original positions.

Duration: 1.2 seconds.

Target: 24 frames at 20 FPS.

The movement should resemble a magical breeze passing through the artwork.

Preserve the original moss texture and avoid obvious rubber-like stretching.

## 5. Experiment C — Central leaf emblem

**Target:** The large green leaf inside the blue stone centrepiece.

This is an experiment in subtle illustrated shape deformation rather than physical movement.

Desired behaviour:

1. Begin with the original leaf shape.
2. The outer edges curl inward slightly.
3. The leaf appears to gather energy.
4. The leaf expands gently, with a subtle organic ripple.
5. It returns to the exact original shape.

Duration: 1.2 seconds.

Target: 24 frames at 20 FPS.

The stone surround must remain completely stationary.

The leaf's painted veins, highlights and shading must remain attached to the deforming surface.

Avoid excessive stretching or visible texture distortion.

If the result looks unnatural, reduce the deformation rather than adding more visual effects.

## 6. Preserve the original artwork

The following elements must remain static during these experiments:

- The large antler structures
- The main bronze frame
- The stone centrepiece
- The three bronze medallions
- The two side leaf panels
- The green tree emblem near the top

Do not animate the entire composition.

Do not regenerate the artwork.

Do not alter the original colour palette, texture style, lighting or proportions.

Animated regions must remain perfectly aligned with their original positions.

Where a moving element reveals previously hidden artwork, reconstruct the exposed background or prepare a clean static plate.

Avoid leaving stationary duplicates beneath animated elements.

## 7. Build an automated bitmap animation pipeline

Create a reproducible pipeline that:

1. Loads the original high-resolution PNG.
2. Identifies the selected animation regions.
3. Produces visual masks and anchor-point previews.
4. Separates moving elements into transparent bitmap layers.
5. Reconstructs any necessary underlying background.
6. Builds deformation meshes with stable anchor points.
7. Defines resting and animated key poses.
8. Interpolates intermediate frames using smooth easing.
9. Renders transparent RGBA PNG sequences.
10. Packs those frames into sprite-sheet atlases.
11. Generates animated GIF or video previews.
12. Generates full-artwork composite previews for visual inspection.

All animations must retain the original artwork's coordinate system.

Support independent animation playback for each motif.

Use deterministic processing so identical inputs and parameters produce identical outputs.

## 8. Animation quality requirements

Prioritise:

- Fluid, organic movement
- Stable painted textures
- Smooth transitions between frames
- Subtle secondary motion
- Natural acceleration and deceleration
- Correct overlap and layering
- Clean transparent edges
- Seamless return to the resting pose

Avoid:

- AI-style morphing
- Texture swimming
- Unnatural stretching
- Wobbling stone or metal
- Duplicated foliage
- Edge halos
- Frame jitter
- Excessive deformation
- Visible snapping when an animation finishes

The result should feel like a professionally animated, high-resolution 2D game asset.

## 9. Output requirements

Create an isolated `animation-rnd` directory.

Organise the output into:

- `source/` — untouched reference artwork
- `masks/` — segmentation masks and previews
- `rigs/` — deformation meshes and anchor configurations
- `animations/` — keyframes and motion parameters
- `frames/` — transparent rendered frame sequences
- `atlases/` — packed sprite sheets
- `previews/` — animated GIF/video and composite previews
- `reports/` — findings and quality assessment

Produce a side-by-side comparison of the original artwork and each animated experiment.

Include a full-composition preview demonstrating how the animated layers integrate with the static artwork.

Export animation frames at sufficient resolution to preserve the original painted detail.

Do not downscale the source unnecessarily.

## 10. Validation and iteration

For each experiment, evaluate:

- Naturalness of movement
- Preservation of original texture
- Quality of segmentation
- Background reconstruction
- Animation smoothness
- Frame consistency
- Seamless resting-pose recovery
- Potential suitability for Epicast

Perform no more than three meaningful refinement passes per experiment.

If automated segmentation is unreliable, report the affected regions and produce an annotated preview for manual review.

Do not conceal poor results using blur, excessive glow or additional particles.

## 11. Constraints

Do not modify Epicast runtime code.

Do not change existing approved artwork.

Do not access or modify SavedVariables.

Do not integrate the animations into WoW yet.

Do not create an animation editor.

Do not attempt to animate every element.

Do not expand the project beyond these three experiments.

## 12. Final deliverables

Provide:

1. Three completed animation experiments.
2. Transparent PNG frame sequences.
3. Sprite-sheet atlases.
4. Animated previews.
5. Full-artwork composite previews.
6. Reproducible scripts.
7. A concise technical findings report.
8. A recommendation for the best production animation technique.

**Stop after completing the experiments and reporting the findings.**

The next phase will be decided after reviewing the actual animation quality.

## Success criteria

The experiments are successful if the original artwork appears to come alive through convincing, high-fidelity 2D animation without losing its painterly appearance.

The final animations should feel like an evolution of MapleStory-style sprite animation, not a static illustration being mechanically distorted.

**Visual quality takes priority over implementation complexity.**
