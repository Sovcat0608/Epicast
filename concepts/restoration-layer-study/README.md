# Restoration crest layer study

Drag **Restoration-layers.svg** onto the Figma canvas and expand its group in the Layers panel. The six named raster layers share a 660 × 880 frame. Toggle **Purple leaves** to see the ivory blades and their contact shading underneath. Toggle **Central sprout** to inspect the recessed green backing.

If your Figma importer flattens the SVG, use the six transparent PNGs in **Restoration-Figma-kit.zip**. Place them in one 660 × 880 frame at X = 0 and Y = 0, with Normal blending and 100% opacity. File 00 is at the back and file 05 is at the front. Each PNG retains its full registration canvas.

| Layer | Contents |
| --- | --- |
| 05 · Crown glyph | Raised dot and crescent, including the dark bevel and contact shade. |
| 04 · Central sprout | Both yellow-green leaves and their shared stem, down to the fork in the wood. |
| 03 · Purple leaves | Both side sprays with their gold veins, the single bottom leaf, and the contact shadow the sprays cast on the ivory below. |
| 02 · Lower ivory leaves | The two pale blades. Their hidden upper portions are reconstructed up to the cuffs. |
| 01 · Upper green leaves | The upper canopy, lateral green leaves, foliage inside the shoulders, and the two small shoots at the middle of the crest. |
| 00 · Carved base | The repainted branches and roots, dark central backing, cuffs, wooden crown, medallion, charms and lower green foliage. |

The approved v8 Restoration painting provides the visible motifs and their registration. Its source is a flattened illustration. Previously concealed bark, green backing, ivory and contact shadows are painted reconstructions. The shoulder branches, central fork and lower root braid have open dark grooves with a lit bark lip for a future emission pass. The review colours approximate Epicast’s 7% Druid desaturation.

**Restoration-preview.png** is the full-resolution assembled stack. **Restoration-layer-guide.png** shows the pieces individually, scaled to fit their tiles. The PNGs and SVG use the same registration. The clipped attachment points on foliage correspond to the resting pose. Large movements would expose those attachment points and change the direction or distance of the painted shadows.
